How to save quotes from books

Keep a passage from a paper book or e-reader, save its author and source, and see it again on your iPhone.

The easiest system is one you can use without losing your place in the book. In Quotify, snap a book page or e-reader screen, check the words and author, and save the passage to your own quote library.

Save a passage from a paper book

  1. Open Photo in Quotify and take a picture of the page, or choose a picture you already took.
  2. Let Smart Photo to Quote find the passage and its author. Keep the page flat and the text well lit.
  3. Review the result before saving. Check punctuation, line breaks and the author's name against the page.
  4. Keep useful context with the quote. A book title and page number in your own fields will make the passage easier to find later.

A photo is a starting point, not a substitute for checking the text. A decorated page, a curved spine or a long passage may need a little editing. For accuracy, capture one passage at a time.

Reading on an e-reader?

The same approach works for a line on a Kindle or another e-reader: photograph the screen. If you are reading in an app on your iPhone, choose a screenshot instead. The Kindle guide explains this workflow and its limits.

Keep a line you hear or find online

For a line read aloud, choose Record and speak the words, then review the transcription. You do not have to wait until you have the book in front of you.

For a passage in Safari, use the share sheet to send it to Quotify. A shared Safari page keeps its link and title, so you can return to the original source. Review the draft rather than assuming the whole page is the quote you meant to save.

Make the saved passage easy to revisit

Mark a passage as a Favorite when it is one you want to keep close. Search your library by its words or author. Add a Home Screen or Lock Screen widget to put saved quotes somewhere you already look, and use Rotation to choose which ones appear.

Start with the lines that made you stop reading. A smaller library of passages you care about is easier to revisit than a folder full of unreviewed page photos.
