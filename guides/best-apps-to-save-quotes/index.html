Best apps to save your own quotes

Compare quote-saving apps by capture, organization and how you will revisit the words you keep.

The best app to save quotes depends on where your lines come from and what you want to do with them afterward. A personal quote library, a reading-highlights importer and a daily quote feed solve different jobs.

Quotify publishes this guide. Checked against each app's App Store listing and docs in November 2026; features change, so check before you choose.

Start with the job you need done

  • Capture: Do your quotes come from paper books, e-readers, websites, conversations or an existing highlight archive?
  • Find: Will you search by words and author, or do you need a larger system of reading notes and sources?
  • Revisit: Would a widget, reminder or dedicated review session help you use what you saved?

When Quotify is a good fit

Quotify suits a library you build a line at a time. Snap a book page or e-reader screen, share from Safari, Instagram or X, or use Record for a line you hear. Check the draft and author, then save it with source context.

The other half of that workflow is seeing the quote again. Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets show your saved quotes, and Rotation lets you choose which part of your library appears. Search, Favorites and your own fields help you keep that collection useful.

When an import-focused app is a better fit

If you already have hundreds of Kindle highlights, start with an app that imports them. Quotify doesn't import Kindle clippings or sync with Amazon. Snapping a page works well for the lines you pick, not for moving a whole archive.

Try a small real collection first

Save a passage from a book, a line from a website and something you heard. Look for them again a few days later. Keep the app that made both steps feel easy.
